\section{Introduction}

College life is so much more than just studying well for a CGPA, 
it is a complicated mess that can be impossible to untangle once you are in it.

Through this paper, I want to untangle one such mess regarding the 
\textbf{open shared space in front of the Sports Block building}. 
Is it the football ground? cricket ground? badminton ground? volleyball ground? 
Or is it simply \textit{a place to connect, evolve, improve, change, play, rest, and do whatever else people want to do?} 
It is interesting how humans are naturally attracted towards open green spaces, where one can simply sit on the grass, walk around, play, or just exist without having a specific reason to be there.\cite{grass}

Through this research, I want to figure out what role this open space in front of the Sports Block plays in an average \textbf{resident's} college life \textit{(because only students arent the focus of this paper).}


\section{Methodology}

The main focus of this paper is to show the results of an observation based approx. 
90min study of the \textbf{open space in front of the Sports Block}. 
I used ethnographic study with semi-structured interviews with people who regularly use the space, 
live there \textit{(guest house)} or people that rarely visit it (more details in the paper below). 

The questions I asked were not just fixed by me, rather they varied based on the responses i.e. how I could branch one question from another, 
allowing the entire convo to remain natural yet meaningful to the study.

\subsection{Picking the Best Time}

The main focus of this study was to analyze and understand the different demographics that visit \textit{the site}, specifically to name it \textbf{the region in front of the Sports Block}.

\textit{The site} is interesting because it is not just a ground with a few goal posts and a volleyball net. 
It has different kinds of spaces within it: stairs, curbs next to the walking path, grass, open areas for sports, a small hill with more sitting 
accommodations basically places where people can sit without necessarily participating in any activity. 

Because of this, different people can use the same physical region (the site) for completely different purposes, as mentioned in a 
\textit{"An Experimental Study of Seating Arrangement Dynamics in Educational Institutions of Delhi" (2017) by Ar. Jahnabi Kalita}\cite{seating}

I also studied a little bit of all 4 times of the day to figure out which is \textbf{the best to focus on}, i.e. since we have 90mins to study I made sure I study the best time for the specific contrast in demographic that I am looking for.

\begin{description}

\raggedright

\item[Morning:] It mainly consisted of observing the people who use the area before classes properly begin. The GYM-people demographic was particularly visible during this time, along with people using the surrounding area for jogging or light exercise.

\item[Afternoon:] The afternoon is a very different situation. The sun is usually extremely bright, especially during the odd semesters, so students usually prefer staying inside the sports block building during this period (playing Table-Tennis, Billiards, Pool and other indoor games). Some exceptions can still be found, such as people playing cricket sometimes or a few day scholars. This makes the afternoon comparatively less active than the evening but still more active than morning.

\item[Evening:] \textbf{The evening is the main focus of this study.} For the 90min observation constraint, the evening provides the best chance to observe different demographics using \textit{the site}. Students are just freed from classes, people come for sports, groups sit, talk and relax, and even other people other than students try to find solace in the evening sunset.

\item[Night:] Nights are alive at IIIT Delhi, but I sadly could not cover this part of the day because of having day-scholar time constraints. Therefore, I will not mention or make any assumptions about the demographic of the ground at night.

\end{description}

\section{Observations}

The main observation was that the ground in front of the Sports Block 
is not really just a sports ground; it is a meeting point 
for many different parts of the IIIT Delhi community. Football, cricket and volleyball 
all at the same place, and the others not interested in sports can use 
the various sitting arangements as mentioned before for completely different reasons. 
Even Profs can be seen using the area for jogging and enjoying with their kids.

Students can occupy the main \textit{flat} part of the ground for sports, and the students 
that are interested in sitting in solace or in groups can occupy the stairs, curbs or the tiny hill
This variety of students, profs, kids of the permanent residents and even security guards was what made the park in the evening so vibrant. 
The space does not have a fixed identity. Its identity changes depending on who is using it.

All these observations made me rethink what any college space actually is for. 
If IIIT Delhi is viewed only through its academic structure, it is easy to 
divide the campus into students, professors and staff. 
But the ground does not operate according to these categories alone its a different
demographic of its own :)). 


\subsection{Social Dynamics}

The social dynamics of the ground are largely informal. There is no single activity controlling the entire space at all times. 
Instead, different activities compete for and share the available physical space.

\section{Conclusions}

The main finding of this study is that the open space in front of the Sports Block is 
much more than a collection of sports facilities. The evening observation showed that 
college life at IIIT Delhi is not limited to students or academic activities. 

Professors can be jogging, their kids can be playing, students can be playing 
football, cricket or volleyball, groups can be found sitting and talking, people can be 
watching activities around them in solitude.

All of these things can happen within the same physical space. 
What makes the ground interesting is therefore not any one activity, 
but the variety of people and ways of living that it manages to accommodate 
at the same time. The ground is not simply infrastructure for campus life; 
it is one of the places where different parts of campus life come together and 
become visible.
